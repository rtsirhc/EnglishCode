On 7 April, Calder Public Library introduced three self-service kiosks at its Riverside Branch, its busiest location. The trial was scheduled to last twelve weeks, although the library board planned to review the results after the first eight. Unlike the old express desk, the kiosks could be used only to borrow or return physical items. Visitors still needed staff assistance to collect reserved books, pay fees, or update membership details.

During the first two weeks, 62% of eligible transactions were completed at a kiosk. However, this figure excluded 276 transactions in which staff had to help after scanners failed to recognise barcodes covered by protective labels. The library did not remove any staff positions. Instead, one part-time assistant was moved from the desk on Friday afternoons to teach visitors how to use the kiosks.

A survey of 89 visitors found that 71 considered the kiosks faster than the traditional desk, while 14 felt there was no difference. The remaining visitors thought the kiosks were slower. Staff members reported fewer short questions at the desk but more complicated requests from visitors who needed help. The director said that no staffing changes would be considered unless at least 70% of eligible transactions were completed independently for three consecutive weeks.
